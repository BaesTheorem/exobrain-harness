# Commercial signage, and the fake-meta list

**Brand data ages.** Chains enter and leave markets. This is a snapshot pulled from
geohints in September 2026. Treat every entry as a prior that shifts probability, never as
a confirmation that ends the search.

The useful discriminator is almost never "I see brand X." It is either a genuinely
single-country brand, or a brand that is **absent from the neighbour's roster**.
TotalEnergies, Shell, BP, Caltex, Puma, Engen, Circle K, Orange, Claro and Airtel span
dozens of countries and tell you close to nothing alone.

## The fake-meta list

Clues the community repeats that are unreliable, out of date, or misread. Each one is worth
knowing precisely because someone will cite it at you.

| The claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Red Google car means Kyrgyzstan" | **Dead.** The current tell is a **white car with a white roof rack**, sometimes only the rack's shadow visible. Red car now means Ukraine or Belgium |
| "Sun in the north means southern hemisphere" | Breaks inside the tropics, when the sun is near overhead, and when it is low enough that direction reads ambiguously. A prior, not a confirmation |
| "Double yellow centre line means the USA" | Canada uses double yellow on wider roads too. Confirm with mph vs km/h signage or plates before committing |
| "White post with a red cap means France" | Poland's colouring is similar; the split is that Poland's red band **wraps fully around** and France's does not. The Baltics copy Polish colouring with slimmer posts |
| "7-Eleven means Japan or Thailand" | 20+ countries including the US, Canada and Mexico. Only useful with the signage language |
| "Gen 2 sky halo pins a country" | It narrows to Gen-2-era regions (North America, Australia, NZ, parts of Western Europe). It eliminates; it does not confirm |
| "No Street View coverage means remote" | In Germany, sparse coverage is a **privacy-law artifact**. Read it as "probably a German city with old opt-out coverage," the opposite of remote |
| "Indonesian pole-tops regionguess reliably" | Plonk It's own words: a "fake meta that kind of works." Real signal, imperfect, shows up where it should not |
| "White flowers mean Estonia" | Genuinely more common in Estonia, especially Gen 3, but found throughout the Baltics |
| "Eucalyptus means Australia" | Planted in Portugal, Brazil, East Africa, California, Spain, Chile. Needs left-hand traffic and soil to become strong |
| "A wide blur means Low Cam" | Taiwan has a wide blur and is not low-mounted. Gen 2's circular blur also gets mistaken for it, and steep inclines fake either |

One more structural trap: **coverage season**. Some low-coverage countries (Kyrgyzstan,
Andorra) were shot almost entirely in one season, so the mental image you build of them is
an artifact of when Google drove, not what the country looks like.
