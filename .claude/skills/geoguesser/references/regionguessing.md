# Regionguessing

Pinning the state, province, prefecture or oblast once the country is fixed. On a
country-locked map this is the whole game, and on the World map it is worth a few hundred
points in a big country.

Prefer **systematic and enumerable** tells (a code on a sign, a numbering scheme, a colour
convention) over impressions. The code tables below are pure memorisation wins.

---

## United States

**Numbering is a coordinate system.** Interstates: even numbers run west-east with low
numbers on the Gulf/Mexico border (I-10); odd run south-north with low numbers on the West
Coast (I-5). US Highways invert this: low odd numbers on the East Coast, low even numbers
near the Canadian border, with more exceptions.

- **Wisconsin and Missouri** are the only two states with lettered county highways (A to
  ZZZ). Wisconsin's signs say "COUNTY" above the letter; Missouri's typically do not.
- **NY and VT county-road mini-signs**: three rows of numbers, top row is the road number.
  Vermont's second row always starts with 0; New York's encodes an NYSDOT district, which
  narrows within the state.
- **I-19** (Tucson to the Mexican border) is the only US highway signed in **kilometres**.
- Area codes are not clustered by leading digit. Genuinely rote.

**Bollards by state** (the densest state-level system in the game): NV thin stick with a
vertical rectangle reflector · CO/WY post extends above a white circular reflector, WY
often painted white or grey on top · WA white or brown post, white rectangle front, two
small squares back · UT white square on a black rectangle · SD/MT white diamond, MT usually
1 bolt and SD 2 · OR long rectangular reflector · UT/WY/NE black paddle-shaped body · NM
three circular reflectors · WI white body with black reflectors (unique) · UT/AZ/ID square
reflector, UT 2 bolts, AZ 1, ID both.

**Utility poles**: CA has 3 yellow stripes at the base (except Modoc and Lassen counties) ·
WI and the Michigan UP use a white rectangular plate with an orange rectangle and diamond ·
PacifiCorp's thick yellow horizontal plates mark OR, eastern ID, UT and WY.

**Guardrail endings**: ME/NH/MA small green-and-red rectangular signs · MN yellow signs with
vertical black triangles before bridges.

**Road surface**: TX (spilling into NM, OK, CO) has a grainy "salt and pepper" texture · CA
puts a black stripe between the double yellow · CA and KY sometimes have no centre line ·
raised pavement markers go on the *side* of the double lines in GA, FL, TX and CA, between
them elsewhere · national parks paint guardrails and posts medium brown.

**Chains as regional markers**: H-E-B, Whataburger, Buc-ee's Texas · Publix Florida and the
Deep South · Waffle House the South (Georgia densest) · Hy-Vee IA/MN/WI/IL · Piggly Wiggly
Southeast plus Wisconsin · Kwik Trip (Kwik Star in Iowa) Upper Midwest · Kum & Go Iowa plus
the Ozarks, CO, WY, ND · Casey's Midwest and Plains · In-N-Out California and the Southwest
· Wawa Mid-Atlantic and Florida · Sheetz Mid-Atlantic and Appalachia · Carl's Jr West Coast
vs Hardee's central and east, overlapping only in WY and OK · Tim Hortons in states
bordering Ontario and Québec.

**Vegetation and crops**: southern pines (loblolly, longleaf) Southeast only · saguaro
southern Arizona · Spanish moss the Deep South · Great Basin sagebrush the arid interior
West · very tall Douglas fir Cascadia, northern CA, ID, MT · **rice only in north-central
California and the Lower Mississippi Valley**, which makes it unusually diagnostic · cotton
coastal Southeast, Lower Mississippi, west Texas, Arizona, parts of California · canola near
the North Dakota border · corn and soy the Corn Belt.

**Architecture**: Pueblo Revival New Mexico (plus AZ, CO) · Spanish Colonial Revival the
desert Southwest plus Florida and coastal Georgia · shingle-wall houses coastal New England
· three-decker triplexes urban NY/CT/RI/MA · brownstones the urban Northeast plus Chicago ·
shotgun houses New Orleans, Houston, Louisville, Mississippi.

**Pole-ID paint is state-coded**: Bahia, Pernambuco and Goiás use long yellow paint with a
black letter code (Goiás starts with a number); São Paulo uses a square yellow plate with a
3-letter municipality code and digits.

**Insulators**: brown "mushroom" is **Pernambuco only** · white mushroom is Bahia, rarely
Brasília · transparent is Alagoas or Santa Catarina · "lollipop" is RN, Paraíba or
Pernambuco.

Moriche means Roraima, Amazonas, Tocantins or Goiás.

**Crops**: coffee dominates Minas Gerais, sugarcane São Paulo, soy Mato Grosso, Paraná and
Goiás. **CEP postcodes** run counterclockwise from São Paulo, ending in Rio Grande do Sul.

## Japan

There is **no clean prefecture code table**. Japanese plates carry a local transport-bureau
name in kanji (品川 Shinagawa, なにわ Naniwa), about 130 of them, not one per prefecture.
Learn by drilling images, not by memorising a table.

**Transformer and pole-top shape is a regional fingerprint**, and this is the systematic
part:

| Region | Pole top |
|---|---|
| Hokkaido | Plus-shaped bar on the pole side; 2 bars or a square with multiple transformers. Never a wooden plate |
| Tohoku | Round plate plus a zigzag bar, or a square with the bar **on top** |
| Kanto | Wooden plate plus 2 bars, or a round plate with a T-bar; 2-3 insulators |
| Hokuriku | Thin metal bars, small "lamp"-shaped insulators, no bar otherwise. Niigata is Tohoku-style despite the region |
| Chubu | Yellow numbering, black tap connectors, large triangle plus horizontal bars |
| Kansai | Straight or zigzag bar on the **bottom** of a square (vs Tohoku's top) |
| Chugoku | Cross-shaped bar plus a full triangle; white sticker with **red** numbers |
| Shikoku | Cross-shaped bar plus a **half** triangle, hat-shaped insulators, white sticker with **black** numbers |
| Kyushu | Straight bar on the side, 3 insulators, transformers 180° apart |
| Okinawa | T-shaped bar on the side, transformers on opposite sides |

**Bollards**: Kyushu/Yamaguchi flat white with orange reflectors on one side · Shikoku black
stripe under the reflector · Tohoku thick white, octagonal reflector, yellow stripe · Akita
two yellow stripes, extendable · Toyama one reflector, extendable, red stripe · Okayama
octagonal reflector, red stripe, sloped base.

**Guardrails**: green bands Oita/Miyazaki/Kyushu · yellow Yamaguchi · chevron stickers
Hokkaido · red-and-yellow striped end-caps Chugoku except Yamaguchi.

**Other**: hexagonal shields mark prefectural roads with the prefecture kanji under the
number, and numbers repeat across prefectures so identify the prefecture first · Hokkaido
has near-exclusive snow-shoulder arrows and vertical traffic lights · tea concentrates in
Shizuoka (36%) and Kagoshima (34%) · rice mainly north of Tokyo · phone area codes increase
going south.

## India

**Plate state codes** (the two-letter prefix):

AN Andaman & Nicobar · AP Andhra Pradesh · AR Arunachal Pradesh · AS Assam · BR Bihar ·
CG Chhattisgarh · CH Chandigarh · DD Dadra & Nagar Haveli / Daman & Diu · DL Delhi ·
GA Goa · GJ Gujarat · HP Himachal Pradesh · HR Haryana · JH Jharkhand · JK Jammu & Kashmir ·
KA Karnataka · KL Kerala · LA Ladakh · LD Lakshadweep · MH Maharashtra · ML Meghalaya ·
MN Manipur · MP Madhya Pradesh · MZ Mizoram · NL Nagaland · OD Odisha · PB Punjab ·
PY Puducherry · RJ Rajasthan · SK Sikkim · TG Telangana · TN Tamil Nadu · TR Tripura ·
UK Uttarakhand · UP Uttar Pradesh · WB West Bengal.

**Script to state is the highest-leverage India tell**: Devanagari means Hindi (north and
centre) or Marathi (Maharashtra, distinguished by **ळ**, absent in Hindi) · Gujarati looks
Devanagari-like **without the top horizontal line** · Gurmukhi (Punjabi) has softer
letterforms and a broken top bar · Bengali means the northeast, shared with Bangladesh ·
Assamese is Bengali script plus **ৰ ৱ** · Telugu adds checkmark diacritics on letter tops
where Kannada does not · Tamil is 90-degree angles plus dot diacritics · Odia has wide curvy
semicircles over the letters · Malayalam is upside-down U shapes · Meitei is vertical lines
and right angles (Manipur) · Mizoram uses Latin · Meghalaya is almost entirely English.

**Poles**: concrete holey poles mean Gujarat, Daman & Diu or Dadra & Nagar Haveli only ·
metal holey poles mean Kerala · poles split into two sections mean Andhra Pradesh or
Telangana · pole stripe **count** is a direct code: 5+ thin black/yellow stripes is Delhi,
exactly 4 thick ones is Bihar.

Yellow tuk-tuks with a black roof mean Telangana, AP or Tamil Nadu. Elevated railways exist
in only eight cities (Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Jaipur, Kochi, Kolkata, Mumbai),
which excludes everywhere else.

*India uses black-and-white striped signposts nationally. There is **no** state-coded
milestone-colour scheme; do not invent one.*

## Australia

- Road prefix **C** appears only in Victoria and Tasmania (a few in SA near the VIC border).
- **130 km/h** speed limit is Northern Territory only.
- **NSW** is the only state with red "No Stopping" signs; **ACT** the only one with text on
  white No Stopping / No Parking signs; **Tasmania** the only one with white directional signs.
- Traffic-light white outline border: WA, QLD, NSW, ACT, TAS. No border: SA, NT, VIC.
- **Stobie pole** (steel and concrete) is South Australia's signature, with a few thousand
  in Tasmania.
- Rusty holed metal poles NT · trident top with red insulators and a black-painted crossbar
  WA · angled-up coil tops QLD · 3-coil tops VIC · L-shaped thin metal crossbar TAS · blue
  circular pole stickers QLD · green-painted pole bases WA · **olive-green possum guards
  Tasmania only** (guards are nationwide, the colour is the tell).
- Stripy black/white bollards WA (unique) · black/yellow bollards QLD (unique) · yellow
  signposts WA (unique) · fluorescent-border road signs almost exclusively QLD.
- Termite mounds mean NT (rare in northern WA and QLD). Darwin has dark red soil with black
  tree trunks. Tasmania reads cool-temperate, comparable to Chile or NZ.

## Canada

- **Ontario** is the only province with a white square at the bottom of km-markers.
- **Alberta rural roads are computable**: Township Roads run east-west with numbers
  increasing north; Range Roads run north-south increasing west and resetting periodically.
  Multiply the grid number by 10 for the road number.
- **Manitoba** likewise: N-roads run east-west and the number is **miles north of the US
  border**; E/W roads run north-south relative to the principal meridian near Winnipeg.
- **Québec** is the only province with "Arrêt" stop signs and the only one with red/white
  chevrons (the rest of Canada uses yellow/black). **New Brunswick** is the only one with
  combined bilingual Stop/Arrêt signs.
- Bollards: Alberta black/white cylindrical with 2 black bands (yellow, blue or green band
  variants) · BC thin curved black/white, Vancouver Island heavy · Québec bollards are
  always attached to guardrails with a red or green reflector top · NWT like Alberta's but
  thicker bands.
- Transformers face the road in BC and New Brunswick, run parallel to the wires elsewhere,
  and sit at 45 degrees in PEI and Nova Scotia. Saskatchewan pole-top insulators alternate
  1-2 / 2-1 from pole to pole.

## France

**The department number is a coordinate.** 96 mainland departments, numbered mostly
alphabetically, and the number appears in two places: the **first two digits of the 5-digit
postcode**, and the **right blue strip of the licence plate**. Phone area codes: 01
Île-de-France, then 02 to 05 clockwise from the north-west; 2X far north increasing
anticlockwise.

**Regional languages each pin a corner**: Breton (Brittany peninsula; d/k/w/ñ heavy,
"Plou-" prefix means north-west Brittany) · Alsatian (Alsace; gasse, strasse) · Occitan
(south; Toulouse *carrièra*, Avignon *carriero*) · Basque (western Pyrénées-Atlantiques;
K/X/Z heavy, thinner sign font, *karrika*) · Corsican (Corsica; Italian-like o→u endings) ·
Catalan (Pyrénées-Orientales only; ç more common than in standard French).

**Signpost colours by region**: burgundy/purple/red the Paris-Dijon corridor · dark green
Brittany, around Paris, and scattered south · Eure-et-Loir green sticker on a dark green
back with superscript D-road numbers (D134.10 style) · greyish-green with a darker post
Côtes-d'Armor, east Ille-et-Vilaine, Pyrénées-Atlantiques · Pas-de-Calais dark green post
with a lighter turquoise back · Seine-et-Marne brown post **and** back · Haute-Garonne
burgundy attachments, sometimes yellow posts.

**City street signs**: Paris green and Prussian blue with a top arch · Lyon four corner
bolts and an uninterrupted white border notched at the bolts · Marseille a Greek-cross
emblem with the border interrupted at the top · Bordeaux a small separate "Arrᵗ" tag ·
Montpellier a blue M top-left · Lille a red fleur-de-lis. Paris, Lyon and Marseille are the
only three cities divided into arrondissements.

## Spain

Regional road markers carry **letter codes by province**, mostly the two-letter province
abbreviation (SG Segovia and so on). National roads use E, AP, A, R and N. Andalusia and
Aragón both reuse "A" for community roads, so that one is ambiguous. Ceuta has N-roads only;
Melilla uses ML.

Phone landlines always start 9, then two regional digits: **two digits gets you the
province**.

**Km-marker colour and shape is an enumerable regional code**: standard tiers are orange >
green > yellow border, with Andalusia using an extra-tall marker with a governing-body
stripe, Madrid a square with the regional flag, and Biscay a larger one with a semicircular
top.

**Regional languages**: Catalan (Catalonia, Valencia, Balearics; à è ò and ç are unique
within Spain, *carrer*, "i" for "and" vs Spanish "y") · Basque (Basque Country and northern
Navarre; K/X/Z heavy, *kalea*) · Galician (close to Portuguese but keeps ñ and drops ç,
*rúa*; town names starting A/O/As/Os are near-unique to Galicia).

**Bollards and building**: green-topped bollard is Extremadura only · square orange
reflector is Lleida/Tarragona plus the eastern Canaries · blue/yellow snow poles Andalusia
only · yellow snow poles Asturias only · slate roofs north-west Spain, the Pyrenees near
Andorra, and the mountains north-east of Madrid · *hórreos* (raised granaries) are Galician
(round or square) or Asturian (bigger, square, 4-pillar, with a 6-8 pillar "panera"
variant; a slate-roofed one means western Asturias).

## Turkey

Three road types on km-markers: **O-roads** (O-hyphen-number), **D-roads** (3 digits with no
visible D, section number on top increasing south-east), and **provincial roads** whose
first two digits are the **provincial code**, numbered alphabetically 01 to 76 (03 is
Afyon). Phone area codes run 2 in the west to 4 in the east; codes starting 5 are
non-geographic.

Yellow/black chevrons mean Balıkesir or Kars; black/yellow means north of Izmir. Tiled roofs
cover the western two-thirds, flat roofs the south-east and south coast, metal roofs the
east, and low-pyramid metal roofs specifically the Lake Van area. Sandstone brick means
Nevşehir. Tea means Rize, cotton around Şanlıurfa. European Turkey's utility poles carry a
distinctive 6-digit code plate, except in Istanbul. City street-sign colours: Ankara blue
with a round top · Izmir dark blue bulging outward with the city emblem · Trabzon and
Yalvaç red oval · Bursa green with extruded letters · Bolu flat green with no border.

## Poland

Three-digit regional road numbers cluster geographically by first digit (two-digit roads do
not). Phone area codes are the first two digits of a 9-digit landline, and even the **first
digit** gives a fast regional cut.

**Place-name suffixes are geography**: **-owo** northern half · **-ów** southern half
(mnemonic: Kraków is south) · **-ino/-yno** north-west · **-ew** centre.

Minority signage: Kashubian in the north (heavy umlauts), German in Opole Voivodeship,
Lithuanian around Puńsk, Belarusian near the border, Lemko in Cyrillic in the south-east.
Pole markings: yellow west and south · black south-east and some north · white background
mostly Podlaskie. Traditional wooden houses mean the east, mainly Podlaskie. Orchards
cluster around Grójec and Sandomierz.

## Southeast Asia

**Indonesia** — Kabupaten (regency) names are the real unit, 417 of them, and they appear on
signage constantly. Postal codes are 5 digits grouped by leading digit from 1 (Jakarta) to 9
(Maluku and Papua). Pole-top shape plus regional add-ons is the densest set: trident means
Central Java or Yogyakarta, an A-frame support means Kalimantan or Nusa, an extra attachment
under the bar means northern or central Sulawesi plus Gorontalo. **Satellite-dish tilt
direction tells you which side of the equator you are on.**

**Malaysia** — each state except the three federal territories has a unique road-number
starting letter (enumerable, though the full key was not captured; Sabah's A2/A7 is the
noted exception). Postal codes appear on most street signs. Trash-bin logos work as regional
markers: an 'S' logo means Negeri Sembilan, Malacca or Johor; a leaf means Perlis or Kedah;
the "Alam Flora" sunny-landscape logo means Pahang, KL or Putrajaya.

**Thailand** — national road **first digit is the region**: 1 north, 2 north-east, 3 centre,
4 south. Clean, though large 1-2 digit highways cross regions. Provincial waystones carry a
2-letter province abbreviation in Thai script. Utility "TCC-L" boxes carry 3-letter province
abbreviations (NTB is Nonthaburi). Bangkok's area code is 02, others are 3 digits.

**Philippines** — no plate-to-province code, but motorcycle plates use a colour-coded strip:
**north has the stripe on top, south on the bottom**. A genuine binary regional split.
Utility-pole boxes carry inconsistent 3-letter municipality abbreviations. Province names on
signage do not appear on the game map, so they need separate memorisation.

## Gaps

Not verified and deliberately omitted rather than guessed: Malaysian state road-letter key
in full, Indonesian plate prefix table, US area-code map (genuinely rote, no rule),
Indian state milestone colours (no such system exists). Mexico, Argentina, Chile and South
Africa have good plonkit coverage not fully pulled here: South Africa's N/R vs A/B vs MR
road-prefix exclusivity and Chile's A-to-Y regional road-letter-by-latitude system are the
headline enumerable ones.
